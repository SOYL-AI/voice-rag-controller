\subsection{E1: Validity of Retrieval-Need Signals}
\label{subsec:e1_signal_validity}
In E1, the main question was whether the model's uncertainty signals could predict whether retrieval was needed. Performance was measured using AUC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve), with a pre-registered success threshold of \( \geq 0.70 \). The results showed that \texttt{max\_entropy\_nats} was the best-performing uncertainty signal, achieving a mean AUC of 0.9784 across five seeds. Therefore, E1 met its pre-registered success criterion.

The seed-level AUC values were 0.9907, 0.9784, 0.9815, 0.9630, and 0.9784 for seeds 11, 23, 37, 53, and 71, respectively. \texttt{max\_entropy\_nats} was the strongest E1 gate signal, whereas \texttt{mean\_entropy\_nats} is the signal used by the downstream controller. Both signals exceeded the frozen AUC threshold of 0.70.

For calibration, \texttt{max\_entropy\_nats} was mapped to the probability that retrieval was needed using a one-dimensional logistic regression model. The fitted mapping was \(P(\text{retrieval needed}) = \sigma(11.7691x - 16.0030)\), which places the 0.5 probability decision boundary at approximately 1.3598 nats. At this threshold, the in-sample classification accuracy was 90.6\%. The calibrated probabilities also produced an AUC of 0.9784; however, because the calibration was fitted in-sample, this value is not treated as additional held-out evidence. The five-seed E1 result remains the evidence used to assess generalization.


\subsection{E2: Value of Retrieval Timing}
\label{subsec:e2_timing}
In E2, we evaluated whether earlier retrieval timing could reduce time-to-first-audio (TTFA) while maintaining retrieval accuracy. The pre-registered primary criterion required the forecast-timed condition, implemented as \texttt{fixed\_early\_proxy}, to achieve a lower p95 TTFA at matched accuracy. Hit@3 accuracy was 100\% across all three timing policies. However, \texttt{fixed\_early\_proxy} recorded a p95 TTFA of 7090 ms compared with 7052 ms for \texttt{sentence\_end}. Since the p95 TTFA was higher rather than lower, E2 did not meet its pre-registered primary criterion.

In the secondary paired-scenario analysis, \texttt{fixed\_early\_proxy} showed a median TTFA difference of approximately -479 ms relative to \texttt{sentence\_end}, with the paired-bootstrap 95\% confidence interval excluding zero. This provides qualified evidence that earlier retrieval placement can reduce latency, but it does not override the primary p95 result. \texttt{fixed\_early\_proxy} was also a fixed early dispatch point in the modeled speech timeline rather than a live VAD/VAP-based forecasting policy.
