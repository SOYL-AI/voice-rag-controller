This work studied how a voice-based RAG system can decide whether retrieval is needed and when retrieval should be started during a conversation. We evaluated a unified controller through three experiments covering retrieval signals, retrieval timing, and the complete controller. In E1, the results showed that model uncertainty can be a useful signal for deciding whether retrieval is needed. Maximum entropy achieved a mean AUC of 0.9784 across five seeds and met the required E1 criterion. E2 showed that retrieval timing can affect response time: all timing methods achieved 100 percent Hit@3 accuracy, and the fixed-early method reduced median response time by 479 ms compared with waiting until the end of the sentence. However, E2 did not meet its pre-registered p95 response-time criterion, so the full timing hypothesis was not confirmed. In E3, Ours achieved strict dominance over the price-aware baseline in 0 out of 5 seeds, below the required 3 out of 5, so the experiments do not show a consistent overall advantage of the proposed controller. The E3 results also indicate a boundary condition where belief saturation can limit the benefit of coupling retrieval and timing decisions. Overall, the findings support the usefulness of uncertainty signals for retrieval and show that retrieval timing can affect response time, while also showing that the complete controller does not consistently improve all evaluated outcomes under the tested conditions. Memory-based scheduling was not evaluated in this work and is left for future work
