We thank both reviewers for careful and constructive reports. Several of your
points changed the paper substantially. Before the item-by-item answers, we
summarise what changed since the version you reviewed.

**1. A correction.** The numbers in Sec. V-C and Figs. 2–3 of the submitted
version came from sample data in our repository, not from the experiment. We
found this while preparing the revision. Every number from that experiment is
now taken from the real sweep (50 scenarios, 26 latency prices, five seeds).
Figs. 2–3 were removed. Their content (frontier coincidence, band counts and
sign agreement) is reported in the text of Sec. VI-A. The revised paper calls
the submitted experiments (E1 belief validation, E2 dispatch timing, E3
controller sweep) together "the text experiment".

**2. A new framing.** Reviewer 1 showed that the tie was predictable, and
Reviewer 2 asked us to keep the coupling null central and say where joint
control could help. Sec. IV now proves that, under three assumptions the text
experiment meets by design (A1–A3), the joint controller at any latency price
makes the same decisions as the decomposed controller at a mapped price
(Prop. 1). It also bounds the gap at a fixed price (Prop. 2) and says which
broken assumption could let joint control pay.

**3. A new, pre-registered streaming experiment.** We added a test on two
larger datasets rendered as speech, with live ASR partials. It was frozen in a
pre-registration before any test data were used. It runs the same items under
two architectures (commit and speculative prefetch) and tests the boundary the
theory predicts (P1–P3), together with a reverse hypothesis, H-REV. We
registered H-REV before the freeze because the development data pointed that
way.

**4. The main result is negative.** On the open-domain set (d1_open, 3,734
test items, scored by alias matching) the joint controller was worse than the
strongest decomposed rival (B9+) under commit: Δ_C = −0.0367
[−0.0481, −0.0252]. H-REV holds (p < 10⁻⁴ after Holm adjustment). P1 and P3
fail, P2 does not survive the Holm correction, and the boundary claim is not
supported. On clean text the gap almost disappears (−0.0024 [−0.0072,
+0.0022]).

**5. Exploratory results.** The customer-support set (d2_support) is graded by
an LLM judge. One human annotator labelled 300 d2_support answers, and the
judge agreed on 80.0% of them (Cohen's κ = 0.55). Because this agreement is
moderate, every d2_support result and every pooled result is reported as
exploratory. This includes the pooled analysis, which was the pre-registered
primary test. Its verdicts match d1_open (Δ_C = −0.0351 [−0.0458, −0.0245]).
d1_open needs no judge.

**6. What we did not do.** We did not run a controller on the maximum-entropy
signal (R2.3). We did not measure the waste rate; the paper uses a placeholder
and reports a sensitivity analysis (R1.10, R2.6). We report TTFA but not a
separate dead-air rate (R1.9). We did not use recorded human speech (R2.2).
These are stated in the paper and in the items below.
