Decide whether the candidate answer correctly answers the caller's question, judged against the reference answer and its KEY FACTS.

The candidate is CORRECT only if BOTH of these hold:
1. It states EVERY key fact listed below. A key fact counts as stated when the candidate gives the same information in any wording, or in an equivalent unit or format (for example "₹50" = "50 rupees" = "Rs 50"; "48 hours" = "two days"; "9 AM" = "09:00"; "13 amp" = "13 A").
2. It contradicts nothing in the reference answer: no figure, time, name, price, place, condition, limit or exception that the reference gives differently.

Otherwise it is INCORRECT. In particular:
- Deferring instead of answering ("please contact us", "check your policy documents", "it depends on your plan", "our team will review it") without stating the key facts is INCORRECT.
- Declining, saying it does not know, or answering a different question is INCORRECT.
- A hedge or generic statement that is consistent with the reference but leaves out a key fact is INCORRECT. Example: "our policy typically covers burst pipes" when the key facts include an exclusion and an excess.
- Extra detail that is true, or that the reference does not contradict, is fine. Wording, length, politeness and naming the company do not matter.

First check each key fact, one per line, as 'KEY FACT: <fact> -> STATED' or 'KEY FACT: <fact> -> MISSING'.
Then list every direct conflict with the reference, one per line, as 'CONFLICT: reference says X, candidate says Y', or write 'CONFLICT: none'.
Then on the final line write exactly 'VERDICT: YES' or 'VERDICT: NO'.
