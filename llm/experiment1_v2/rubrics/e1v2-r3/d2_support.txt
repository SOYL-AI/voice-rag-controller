Decide whether the candidate answer correctly answers the caller's question, judged against the reference answer, its CORE FACTS and its SUPPLEMENTARY FACTS.

The candidate is CORRECT only if BOTH of these hold:
1. It states EVERY core fact listed below. A core fact counts as stated when the candidate gives the same information in any wording, in an equivalent unit or format, or as a close approximation (for example "₹50" = "50 rupees" = "Rs 50" = "about ₹50"; "48 hours" = "two days"; "9 AM" = "09:00"; "13 amp" = "13 A").
2. It contradicts nothing in the reference answer or in the supplementary facts: no figure, time, name, price, place, condition, limit or exception that they give differently. Supplementary facts MAY be omitted; they must not be contradicted.

Otherwise it is INCORRECT. In particular:
- Deferring instead of answering ("please contact us", "check your policy documents", "it depends on your plan", "our team will review it") without stating the core facts is INCORRECT.
- Declining, saying it does not know, or answering a different question is INCORRECT.
- A hedge or generic statement that leaves out a core fact is INCORRECT, even if nothing it says is false. Example: "our policy typically covers burst pipes" when a core fact is the excess that applies.
- Extra detail that is true, or that the reference does not contradict, is fine. Wording, length, politeness and naming the company do not matter.

First check each core fact, one per line, as 'CORE FACT: <fact> -> STATED' or 'CORE FACT: <fact> -> MISSING'.
Then list every direct conflict with the reference or a supplementary fact, one per line, as 'CONFLICT: reference says X, candidate says Y', or write 'CONFLICT: none'.
Then on the final line write exactly 'VERDICT: YES' or 'VERDICT: NO'.
